Forthcoming

With Clément de Chaisemartin · Princeton University Press, forthcoming

Previous version (SSRN)

This textbook offers an up-to-date survey on difference-in-differences. Identification and Estimation of average marginal effects in fixed effect logit models

With Laurent Davezies, Louise Laage · under revision at Review of Economic Studies

Show abstractHide abstract

We show that the sharp bounds on the average marginal and treatment effects (AME and ATE) can be obtained very simply in fixed effect logit models, without any optimization. We consider two related estimation methods. The second is very simple but yields asymptotically larger confidence intervals, though it performs very well in finite samples.

Is Inference Conditional on Not Rejecting a Pre-test Less Reliable than Unconditional Inference?

With Clément de Chaisemartin · conditionally accepted at American Economic Review

Show abstractHide abstract

The common practice of conducting inference on a parameter of interest conditional on not rejecting a specification test is valid (though generally conservative) if the tested conditions hold, under general conditions. In particular, this requires no assumption on the asymptotic dependence between the estimator of the parameter of interest and the specification test. Conditional inference may also have better properties than unconditional inference under local alternatives.

Instrument-Free Demand Estimation Using Relative Prices Variation, with an Application to Railway Transportation

With Philippe Février, Ao Wang, Lionel Wilner · revised and resubmitted to Quantitative Economics

Show abstractHide abstract

We quantify gains and losses from revenue management relative to uniform and flexible pricing using French railway data. The analysis combines temporal variation in relative prices with restrictions on consumer behavior and firms’ pricing decisions to identify demand and evaluate alternative strategies.

Analytic inference with two-way clustering

With Laurent Davezies, Yannick Guyonvarch · under revision at American Economic Review

Show abstractHide abstract

The usual analytic variance estimator for two-way clustering may not be positive and is invalid for some DGPs. We propose a simple fix that solves both issues. When the estimator of the parameter of interest is asymptotically Gaussian, the corresponding tests are asymptotically exact and equivalent to usual ones. Otherwise, the new tests are asymptotically conservative.

Difference-in-Differences for Continuous Treatments and Instruments with Stayers

With Clément de Chaisemartin, Félix Pasquier, Gonzalo Vazquez-Bare, Doulo Sow · under revision at Quantitative Economics

Show abstractHide abstract

We propose new DID estimators for continuous treatments. We also assume that from two periods, the treatment of some units, the movers, changes, while the treatment of other units, the stayers, does not change. Then, our estimators compare the outcome evolution of movers and stayers with the same initial value of the treatment. Our estimator is robust to heterogeneous treatment effects. We also extend this framework to an IV setup.

Difference-in-Differences Estimators When No Unit Remains Untreated

With Clément de Chaisemartin, Diego Cicca, Felix Knau

Show abstractHide abstract

We consider treatment-effect estimation in designs in which no unit is treated initially, and then units simultaneously receive heterogeneous and strictly positive treatment doses. Under a parallel-trends assumption and quasi-untreated units, a causal effect is identified by an estimand mixing DID and RDD. We also consider designs without quasi-untreated units and propose a test of the homogeneous-effect assumption underlying TWFE regressions.

Linear Regressions with Combined Data

With Christophe Gaillac, Arnaud Maurel

Show abstractHide abstract

We consider linear regressions when the outcome is not observed in the same dataset as some of the covariates. Without further restriction, we show that the coefficients are partially identified and the bounds take a very simple form. We also show their asymptotic normality.

Price Discrimination and Online Sales in the Automobile Industry

With Isis Durrmeyer, Jean-François Fournel, Alessandro Iaria

Show abstractHide abstract

We investigate the consequences of introducing an online distribution channel in the French car industry, where currently most of the sales take place in person through car dealers relying on third-degree price discrimination. To this end, we develop a structural model of demand with unobserved third-degree price discrimination and transportation costs related to visiting car dealers.

Empirical MSE Minimization to Estimate a Scalar Parameter

With Clément de Chaisemartin

Show abstractHide abstract

We consider the estimation of a scalar parameter, when two estimators are available. The first is always consistent. The second is inconsistent in general, but has a smaller asymptotic variance than the first, and may be consistent if an assumption is satisfied. We propose to use the weighted sum of the two estimators with the lowest estimated mean-squared error (MSE). This third estimator dominates the other two from a minimax-regret perspective.

Treatment-Effect Estimation in Complex Designs under a Parallel-trends Assumption

Forthcoming

With Clément de Chaisemartin · forthcoming in Econometric Society Monograph (13th World Congress)

Show abstractHide abstract

The paper studies identification of dynamic treatment effects under parallel trends, with possibly non-binary and non-absorbing treatment. The first part clarifies which event-study effects are identified without further restrictions. Those effects can be helpful for ex post analysis, but cannot be used for ex ante analysis. Then, we consider another restriction, namely a random coefficients distributed-lag linear model. We illustrate our findings by revisiting Gentzkow et al. (2011).

Difference-in-Differences Estimators of Intertemporal Treatment Effects

2026

With Clément de Chaisemartin · Review of Economics and Statistics, 108 (2026)

Working paper version

Show abstractHide abstract

We consider treatment effect estimators with a panel of groups under parallel trends. We propose event-study estimators of the effect of being exposed to a weakly higher treatment dose for ℓ periods. We also propose normalized estimators, that estimate a weighted average of the effects of the current treatment and its lags. Contrary to usual estimators, they are valid even if the treatment effect is heterogeneous and beyond staggered adoption designs.

A Robust Permutation Test for Subvector Inference in Linear Regressions

2024

With Pujee Tuvaandorj · Quantitative Economics, 15 (2024)

Working paper version

Show abstractHide abstract

We develop a new permutation test for inference on a subvector of coefficients in linear models. The test is exact when the regressors W and the error terms u are independent, but also consistent under, mainly, two conditions: (i) a slight reinforcement of cov(W, u)=0; (ii) the number of strata, defined by values of the regressors not involved in the subvector test, is small compared to the sample size.

Fixed Effects Binary Choice Models with Three or More Periods

2023

With Laurent Davezies, Martin Mugnier · Quantitative Economics, 14 (2023)

Working paper version

Show abstractHide abstract

We show that the impossibility result of Chamberlain for fixed effects binary choice models (with iid errors, the slope parameter is point identified only with logistic errors) only holds with T=2. With three or more periods, we exhibit a family of distributions for which point identification can be achieved. Identification leads to a GMM estimator, which reaches the semiparametric efficiency bound when T=3. We apply our method to revisit Brender and Drazen (2008).

An alternative to synthetic control for models with many covariates under sparsity

2023

With Marianne Bléhaut, Jérémy L’Hour, Alexandre Tsybakov · in Belomestny, D. et al. (eds) Foundations of Modern Statistics. Springer (2023)

Working paper version

Show abstractHide abstract

The synthetic control program does not admit a unique solution when n, the number of control units, is larger than p, the dimension of the variables used to construct the synthetic control. We propose a method that solves this issue and enjoys a double robustness property. We then extend it to the case where p is potentially larger than n, and show root-n consistency and asymptotic normality of the corresponding ATT estimator.

Fuzzy Differences-in-Differences

2018

With Clément de Chaisemartin · Review of Economic Studies, 85 (2018)

Working paper version

Show abstractHide abstract

In many applications of the DID method, the treatment rate only increases more in the treatment group. In such fuzzy designs, we show that the popular ‘Wald-DID’ (the DID of the outcome divided by the DID of the treatment) identifies a LATE only if two homogeneous treatment effect assumptions hold. We then propose two alternative estimands that do not rely on such assumptions.

Measuring Segregation on Small Units: A Partial Identification Analysis

2017

With Roland Rathelot · Quantitative Economics, 8 (2017)

Working paper version

Show abstractHide abstract

Suppose that an individual in a small unit j (a classroom, a small firm…) belongs to a minority with a probability pj. To measure segregation of this minority, one would ideally use an inequality index on the pj, but they are unobserved. Using the observed proportion instead leads to an overestimation. The segregation indices are actually partially identified. We provide tractable bounds and develop inference.

Identification of Nonseparable Models with Endogeneity and Discrete Instruments

2015

With Philippe Février · Econometrica, 83 (2015)

Working paper version

Show abstractHide abstract

Consider a model Y = g(X,U) with X endogenous, and suppose that we have instruments Z such that X = h(Z,V). If Z is independent of (U,V) and both g(X,.) and h(Z,.) are strictly monotonic, then g can be partially or point identified if Z is binary.
